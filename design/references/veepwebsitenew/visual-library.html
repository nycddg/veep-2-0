Visual grammar

A job has three ownership states. An ownerless job has a coral dot inside a dashed ink square. An internal or other non-Veep owner gives the square a solid ink outline around the coral dot. A Veep-owned job has a filled dark square with a blue dot. Use the same size and position for the dot through a transfer. Enclose work only when the boundary explains inclusion, exclusion or a limit. Let spacing and connectors group related marks. Circles identify people. A circled check records expert review. A checked square records accepted delivery.

OwnerlessInternal or other ownerVeep-owned

A circle around a blue square identifies a Veep operator. A square around a dot identifies a job. Use Fund for the investment fund, Portfolio for its companies as a group, and Roster for available operators. When two companies commission different jobs, label each company and its mandate.

Use fine solid rules for context, dashed edges for possible or unassigned work, a solid boundary for agreed scope and a capped line for delivery. A blue segment belongs to a named Veep owner. A loop shows recurrence or revision. State its purpose in the caption. Neutral dividers separate items.

Asset release

Use the marks as the shared vocabulary. The motion catalog is open to new studies. Check new work against the visual grammar before publishing it. Use the downloadable SVG as the master for a mark.
